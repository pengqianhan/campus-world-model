# Art bible: Structures Hall

What the building looks like, for whoever draws a part of it. The photograph is a reference: no size is measured
from it but the ones its contract names, which are estimated.

- Two long blocks side by side, each under a roof of one slope that rises to the line between them; the northern
  one's roof stands 2.2 m over the southern one's there. Three square stacks stand on that line, 15 m apart: a light
  grey band at the top, dark louvres under it, a light grey base. A lower block stands against the east end.
- The south face, the one the photograph sees: three storeys glazed in blue reflecting glass, in a grid of dark
  mullions, with a light grey spandrel panel at each floor and light grey panels among the glass; a light grey fascia
  along the roof edge, standing a little proud of the glass; a dark brown frame at each end of the face.
- Under the glazing, the ground storey set back: grey walls with dark windows and doors, behind plain light piers and
  pairs of diagonal braces that fall from the glazing's underside at two piers to the ground between them, a V.
- The light: a clear afternoon, the sun low and to the north-north-west, behind the building; the glass reflecting the sky and the buildings across
  the lot, the fascia and the panels pale.
