# Art bible: Sir Owen G Glenn Building (260)

What the building looks like, for whoever draws a part of it. The photograph is a reference: no size is measured from
it but the heights of the curved wing's bands of faces, which are estimated.

- A curved wing of several storeys over an open ground floor of round columns, its inner face a curtain wall of
  glass behind horizontal louvres that read blue, between white spandrel bands at each floor; a crown of louvres
  along its top.
- The wing's north arm, close on the photograph's right, is deeper: balconies and steel posts before its glass,
  louvres in tiers.
- In the curve's inner corner, a sloping canopy of striped louvres, low along its front edge and rising into the
  curve, over a glazed ground floor.
- Before it a paved plaza with a raised lawn in a low concrete kerb, and parking lines on its north part.
- The back, over the gully (the photograph's inset): darker, stepped floors.
- The light: a clear summer morning, the sky blue with white cloud, the glass taking the sky's light.
