# Brief: look agent

<!-- Sent as-is to the look agent at the start of round 1 (Clock Tower, 2026-10-20), with local paths rewritten for `landmarks/clocktower/`. Model: the lead's (Claude Opus); agent type: general-purpose. -->

You are the **look** agent on a small team building the University of Auckland Clock Tower in three.js from code alone, checked against Street View photographs. Project folder: `landmarks/clocktower/` in this repository; paths below are relative to it (do not commit — the lead commits). Other agents edit the building's parts at the same time. **You own `src/main.js`, `src/textures.js`, new `src/look/*.js` and a new `src/landscape.js`. Edit nothing else.**

**Read first:** `docs/CONTRACT.md` (frame, sizes, ownership, how to render and compare, budget, rules) and `docs/ART_BIBLE.md` (especially "The light" and "Materials"). Then read `src/main.js`, `src/textures.js`, `src/gothic.js` (how materials are used: one merged mesh per material name, UVs in metres) and `src/clocktower.js`.

**Your job: make the picture look like the photographs** — light, sky, stone, glass, and the street and planting round the building. Study `refs/streetview/*_wide.jpg` (12 views from Princes St, July 2024 morning, sun low in the east **behind** the building, so the front is in open shade under a clear blue sky; some views look toward the sun and are backlit) and the `*_door.jpg` / `*_tower.jpg` close-ups.

1. **Light and sky**: match the photographs' light — sun direction and height for a July morning in Auckland (−36.85°, 174.77°; the street front faces bearing ~289°, i.e. x = −u where u runs at bearing 19°), sky colour gradient, the cool open-shade on the front, rim-lit tower edges. Tone mapping, exposure and contrast set by **measurement**: compare luminance percentiles (p02/p10/p50/p90/p98) and mean colour of the building region in a reference and its render (crop the same region of both with PIL). Consider screen-space ambient occlusion (three's `GTAOPass`/`SAOPass` via `EffectComposer` or the renderer's effects API in r186) for the deep reveals and joints — behind a `?quality=` option so phones can turn it off. Shadows sized to the building.
2. **Materials** (keep the material names: `ashlar`, `smooth`, `dressed`, `glass`, `timber`, `roof`, `iron`, `dial`, `shade` — the parts use them): limestone colour and texture matched to the photographs (rock-faced on the pavilions, finely jointed smooth stone on the centre and tower, dressed stone for mouldings); **weathering** in world space (darker grime low down and in joints, faint streaks under ledges, lichen speckles) via shader hooks (`onBeforeCompile`) or generated maps; glass that reflects the sky and trees with hints of interior, never flat black. Keep generated textures ≤ 2048² and few.
3. **Landscape** (`src/landscape.js`, called from main.js): the real street layout from the photographs and the aerial view (`data/clocktower_aerial.tif` is a 6 cm aerial crop; `data/clocktower_heightmap_vs_aerial.jpg` shows the building against it; ground heights from the LiDAR points `clocktower_pts.npy`, class 2, frame in `clocktower_frame.json`): footpath, kerbs, Princes St with its lane markings and parking, the lawn strip, the planting bed of shrubs and flax along the base; **trees** — a large bare deciduous tree on the right, dark conifers and broadleaf trees on the left and behind (procedural, instanced, cheap); a lamp post and plain parking signposts. Nothing traced from the photographs; no people or cars.

Work in rounds: change → `node tools/compare.mjs --out out/look/rNN --match wide` (and some `--match door`/`tower`) → open the `*_strip.jpg` (reference | render | overlay), measure the luminance/colour → next change. Another agent refines the reference cameras (`views_refined.json` may appear and be used automatically); the other agents change the building's shape — judge light and materials, not shape.

Budget: the page must stay usable in a browser: landscape ≤ ~150k triangles and ≤ ~20 draw calls; total textures few and ≤ 2048²; post effects optional by quality. No Math.random. Do as many rounds as it keeps getting better (aim for 6–10). Finish with a short report: what you built, files, the measured luminance percentiles reference vs render before/after for 3 views, triangles/draw calls, paths of before strips (`out/lead/baseline/`) and best after strips, what still differs, what you'd do next, and anything the shape agents should know (e.g. material names you added).
