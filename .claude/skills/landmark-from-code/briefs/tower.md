# Brief: tower agent

<!-- Sent as-is to the tower agent at the start of round 1 (Clock Tower, 2026-10-20), with local paths rewritten for `landmarks/clocktower/`. Model: the lead's (Claude Opus); agent type: general-purpose. -->

You are the **tower** agent on a small team building the University of Auckland Clock Tower in three.js from code alone, checked against Street View photographs. Project folder: `landmarks/clocktower/` in this repository; paths below are relative to it (do not commit — the lead commits). Other agents edit other files at the same time. **You own `src/parts/tower.js` and any new `src/parts/tower_*.js`. Edit nothing else.**

**Read first:** `docs/CONTRACT.md` (frame, sizes, ownership, how to render and compare, budget, rules) and `docs/ART_BIBLE.md`. Then read `src/parts/tower.js`, `src/parts/common.js` (sizes, read-only), `src/gothic.js` (the shared kit: `Builder`, `addWindow`, `addPinnacle`, `addCourse`, `outline` — you may use it, not edit it; write new general parts in your own files) and `src/clocktower.js`.

**Your job: make the tower read as the real one.** It is the most intricate and most visible part. Study the 12 tower close-ups `refs/streetview/*_tower.jpg` at full size (crop and zoom into them with PIL as you need), plus the `*_wide.jpg` views. What is there now is a first sketch: a square lower stage with four octagonal turrets, a plain octagonal shaft with one louvred opening and a clock per face, a simple crown. The real tower has, roughly (verify everything against the photographs): a lower stage whose roofline bristles with many crocketed pinnacles and gablets; an upper shaft with strong vertical ribs/buttresses at the corners, deep pointed openings with tracery, long blind-tracery panels, the clock dials set in decorated frames; and a crown of open-work: pinnacles at each corner joined by flying ribs/arches, with a short spirelet. Get the **silhouette** right first (it is seen against the sky: count the pinnacles, their heights and spacing, the widths of each stage), then the main relief, then the finer tracery.

Measure, don't guess: LiDAR points of the building are in `data/clocktower_pts.npy` (columns x, y, z, class in local NZTM metres; class 6 = building; the frame transform is in `clocktower_frame.json`: tower centre `centre`, `axis` = −x of the view frame, `toStreet` = +z, ground 45.8 m). Use it to check the tower's plan size at several heights, the heights of the stage roofline, the shaft top and the spire (40.8 m above the ground). The LiDAR is sparse on vertical faces, so use it for heights and plan extents and the photographs for everything else. If a size in `common.js` is wrong, report it with numbers (you can use your own constants in tower.js meanwhile).

Work in rounds: change → `node tools/compare.mjs --out out/tower/rNN --match tower --query "only=tower,centre,pavilions"` → open the `*_strip.jpg` files (reference | render | overlay) → compare closely, crop regions → next change. Also check one or two `--match wide` views and a close orbit shot (`node tools/shot.mjs out/tower/close.png "?pos=12,22,22&look=0,30,0&fov=40" 1200x1600`) so it looks right from angles there are no photos for. Note: another agent is refining the reference cameras (`views_refined.json` may appear and be used automatically) and the look agent is changing materials and light — judge shape, not light.

Budget: the whole tower ≤ ~150k triangles and ≤ ~150 ms build (the console logs `part tower: N ms`; the compare tool prints triangle counts). Use the materials that exist (`smooth` for walls, `dressed` for mouldings/tracery/pinnacles, `iron`, `dial`, `shade`, `glass`, `roof`); ask for a new one in your report if needed. Determinism: no Math.random.

Do as many rounds as it keeps getting better (aim for 6–10). Finish with a short report: what you built, files, triangles and build ms, the paths of a before strip (from `out/lead/baseline/`) and your best after strips, measurements you took, what still differs and what you'd do next.
