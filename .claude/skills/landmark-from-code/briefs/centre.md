You are the **centre** agent on a small team building the University of Auckland Clock Tower in three.js from code alone, checked against Street View photographs. Project folder: `landmarks/clocktower/` in this repository; paths below are relative to it (do not commit — the lead commits). Other agents edit other files at the same time. **You own `src/parts/centre.js` and any new `src/parts/centre_*.js`. Edit nothing else.**

**Read first:** `docs/CONTRACT.md` (frame, sizes, ownership, how to render and compare, budget, rules) and `docs/ART_BIBLE.md`. Then read `src/parts/centre.js`, `src/parts/common.js` (sizes, read-only), `src/gothic.js` (the shared kit: `Builder`, `addWall`, `addWindow`, `addPinnacle`, `addCourse`, `outline` — you may use it, not edit it; write new general parts in your own files) and `src/clocktower.js`.

**Your job: make the recessed centre between the two gabled pavilions read as the real one** — the richest part of the front at eye level. Study the 12 doorway close-ups `refs/streetview/*_door.jpg` at full size (crop and zoom with PIL as needed) and the `*_wide.jpg` views. What is there now is a first sketch. The real centre has, roughly (verify everything against the photographs): a deep pointed doorway with a moulded arch and carved timber tracery in its head over open timber doors; a broad flight of steps between cheek walls that end in tall square pedestals near the footpath; a flat-roofed porch on slender posts to one side of the steps; above the door an ornate canopied/corbelled panel and a projecting oriel-like window; two- and three-light pointed windows on three floors with tracery heads; slim buttress-shafts running up the bay and through a pierced or panelled parapet into crocketed pinnacles; string courses; corbels and carved bosses. The building's number plate and bins/signs at the base are not part of the building. Get the big masses and the depth (what projects, what is recessed, by how much) right first, then the openings, then the carving.

Measure, don't guess: LiDAR points are in `data/clocktower_pts.npy` (x, y, z, class in local NZTM metres; class 6 = building, 2 = ground; frame in `clocktower_frame.json`: tower centre `centre`, `axis` = −x of the view frame, `toStreet` = +z, ground 45.8 m). Use it to check the bay's width, its front plane (now z = 12.5), the parapet height (now 11.0) and the steps/porch footprint; the LiDAR is sparse on walls, so take facade details from the photographs. If a size in `common.js` looks wrong, report it with numbers (use your own constants in centre.js meanwhile).

Work in rounds: change → `node tools/compare.mjs --out out/centre/rNN --match door --query "only=centre,pavilions,tower"` → open the `*_strip.jpg` (reference | render | overlay) → compare closely, crop regions → next change. Check some `--match wide` views too, and a close shot from an angle without photos (e.g. `node tools/shot.mjs out/centre/close.png "?pos=-6,3,24&look=0,5,12&fov=50" 1600x1067`). Another agent is refining the reference cameras (`views_refined.json` may appear and be used automatically) and the look agent is changing materials and light — judge shape, not light.

Budget: the centre ≤ ~100k triangles, ≤ ~100 ms build (console logs `part centre: N ms`). Use existing materials (`smooth` walls, `dressed` mouldings and carving, `timber`, `glass`, `iron`, `roof`, `shade`); ask for a new one in your report if needed. No Math.random.

Do as many rounds as it keeps getting better (aim for 6–10). Finish with a short report: what you built, files, triangles and build ms, paths of a before strip (`out/lead/baseline/`) and your best after strips, measurements taken, what still differs, what you'd do next.
