# Changelog

What changed on the site, newest first. Every merge into `main` is published straight away, so
entries are dated (New Zealand time) rather than numbered as versions. Numbers in brackets are pull
requests.

A change to what the site does or shows adds its line here, and the README's
[What's new](README.md#whats-new) says it in a few words (see [docs/standards.md](docs/standards.md)).

## Held back

- **Course catalogue.** Search for all 6,982 courses of the 2026 Curriculum Catalogue by code or
  title, with the terms and campuses each is offered in (#24). Built, and kept out of `main` until
  the Classes API key arrives, so that what the timetable and the catalogue each supply can be
  settled with both in hand.

## 2026-11-03

### Changed

- **Buildings drawn in detail side by side stand on one ground.** Where two buildings drawn from code
  touch, they now stand at the heights the LiDAR gives them against each other, on a ground they share,
  instead of each on the map's own ground at its door, which is a metre or more off the LiDAR's and by a
  different amount at each door: 9 Eden Crescent stands 0.45 m higher than before and 15 Eden Crescent
  0.45 m lower, the Thomas Building 0.31 m lower and its Extension 0.31 m higher, and the Thomas
  Building's parapet runs on under its Extension's glass link again. Where the map's ground is too far
  off the LiDAR's at one of the two doors, as at the Clock Tower's, the two stand as before, and so does
  every other building (#132).

### Fixed

- **No more flicker where two faces of a landmark met.** In twelve of the landmarks drawn in detail, faces
  of two materials lay in one plane over the same patch: a wall's face in its roof slab's side, a pane's
  edge in its frame's, a plinth's top in its moulding's, the backs of glass and frames on one plane. The
  screen cannot tell which of two such faces is in front, so they flickered as the camera moved, on the map
  and from a walker's height. Now the hidden face of each pair stops short, stands inside its neighbour, or
  stands a centimetre back, and what shows stands where it stood, to the centimetre. One thing shown is gone:
  across Maclaurin Chapel's flat roof, where the near end's roof met the walk-ways', a white edge and its
  dark line rose through the two roofs, which are at one height, and showed from above as a dark band 12 cm
  wide; the edge now stops under the roof, white, with no dark line, and closes the space under it as
  before. A test checks every landmark on the site for faces closer than the map can tell apart (#131).
